Frequently Asked Questions

FAQs — BiCS 2026

Everything you need to know about participation, submission, and presentation at the Blue Intelligence Climate Summit 2026.

How to Participate — Students (Ages 18–25)
1
Form Your Team
Gather a team of 2 to 4 eligible students. Cross-disciplinary teams (e.g., Data Science paired with Marine Biology) are highly encouraged.
2
Select Your Track
Choose from The Smart Port Blueprint, The Eco-Pulse Concept, or The Civic Resilience Map.
3
Submit Registration
Fill out the student portal form with your abstract concept, design draft link, and college identity proofs as per the registration deadline details.
4
Mount Your Poster
Arrive on Day 1 at 14:00 to receive your designated exhibition board space, display materials, and mentor feedback.
Student Poster Presentation FAQs

Process of Submission & Scope
No. All abstract submissions must represent original, unpublished work that is not under consideration by any other conference, summit, or journal.
Yes. The core mandate of BICS 2026 is digital intelligence. Your methodology must explicitly leverage machine learning, computer vision, neural networks, big data architectures, or advanced computational modeling to solve a distinct marine or coastal challenge.
A researcher can be listed as a co-author on multiple papers, but can only act as the primary/corresponding author for one abstract submission.
Review Process & Publication
Submissions undergo a double-blind peer-review process managed by our academic committee. Grading is based strictly on scientific rigor, methodology validity, data accuracy, and practical environmental impact.
The top-ranked papers in each track will be directly recommended to our partner journal editors (including Marine Policy and Applied Ocean Research) for fast-tracked peer review and open-access publication.
For the Grand Winner (₹2,00,000 prize), BICS 2026 fully funds the open-access publication fee. Other recommended papers may be subject to the standard processing charges of the respective journal.
Presentation Logistics
Yes. At least one author from each accepted paper must register and attend the summit in Visakhapatnam to present their findings during the Day 2 Technical Sessions.
The Best Paper Award winner receives a full travel grant. Limited partial travel stipends are available for early-career researchers and PhD candidates on a merit-backed request system.
Time Allocation
Duration per poster per student: 10 minutes only. Plan your pitch and Q&A accordingly.
Dimensions & Materials
Standard Dimensions
  • Size: A0 Portrait Orientation (strictly)
  • Width: 841 mm (33.1 inches)
  • Height: 1189 mm (46.8 inches)
Paper & Mounting
  • Paper Quality: Minimum 200 GSM, Matte or Satin finish
  • Glossy finishes: Heavily discouraged (glare from spot lighting)
  • Mounting: Venue provides boards & heavy-duty tape — do not frame
Visual Layout & Typography Rules
Typography
  • Title: Minimum 72 pt Bold — team name, track & institution at 36 pt below
  • Body text: Minimum 24 pt — readable from 1.5 metres
  • Section headers: Minimum 40 pt
Content Balance
  • 60% Visual: High-res flowcharts, wireframes, maps, graphs
  • 40% Text: Punchy bullet points — avoid dense paragraphs
  • BiCS 2026 Logo: Top-right corner (mandatory)
  • Bottom-right: Reserve 10×10 cm for Exhibition Board Number
Judging Evaluation Rubric — Day 2 Gallery Review

Judges will score each team out of a maximum of 50 points across five core metrics on their digital evaluation tablets.

1
Technological Innovation & AI Logic
10 pts
Criteria: How effectively does the poster deploy digital intelligence (AI, ML, UI/UX, or Data Architecture) to address the track prompt? Is the technical logic sound or just buzzwords?
9–10: Exceptional tech model with clear data flows 6–8: Practical application of existing tools 1–5: Weak technical depth or purely theoretical
2
Practical Feasibility & Scalability
10 pts
Criteria: Can this blueprint be realistically developed and executed by shipping lines, eco-tourism resorts, or local municipal bodies, or is it too speculative?
9–10: Highly viable, immediate deployment potential 6–8: Feasible but requires significant infrastructure changes 1–5: Highly unrealistic or cost-prohibitive
3
Visual Layout & Data Design
10 pts
Criteria: Is the poster visually engaging and balanced? Do the charts, system diagrams, and maps accelerate understanding, or is the board cluttered and text-heavy?
9–10: Flawless layout, beautiful data graphics 6–8: Clear organisation but slightly text-dense 1–5: Messy layout, low-res images, or tiny fonts
4
Cross-Disciplinary Alignment
10 pts
Criteria: How well does the concept bridge the gap between technical data science and core marine/maritime realities? Does it show a deep understanding of ocean constraints?
9–10: Perfect blend of data science & ocean climate realities 6–8: Strong solution but slightly disconnected from marine nuances 1–5: Fails to address oceanographic or logistical constraints
5
Pitch Presentation & Q&A Defensibility
10 pts
Criteria: How confidently does the team articulate their design to the jury within the strict 3-minute pitch window? How accurately do they answer technical cross-examinations?
9–10: Highly articulate, structured pitch & authoritative answers 6–8: Clear presentation but struggles with deep technical counters 1–5: Unprepared, reads directly off the poster
